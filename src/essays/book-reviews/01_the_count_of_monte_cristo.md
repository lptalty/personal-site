"And you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free." (John 8:32)

When you first pick up the Penguin Classics edition of the tome that is The Count of Monte Cristo you will meet a back cover that reads: "The epic tale of wrongful imprisonment, adventure and revenge, in its definitive translation." This misrepresents Dumas' classic, though, as this book is not about revenge. Instead, it is a story of forgiveness, repentence, and faith. 

The initial wrongful imprisonment, and subsequent plot for revenge, only serves as a catalyst for Dantes' spiritual liberation-a transformation beyond the confines of the material world in two parts: first into the worldy Count of Monte Cristo, an international multi-millionaire and master of disguise, and then, finally, into a faithful servant of God. Edmond Dantes was born a prisoner to sin. His incarceration saved his soul ('As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good' Genesis 50:20)

# first transformation: a god among men
in the cave, abbe faria, caderousse death which was unplanned. He did want revenge. He wanted to be as God is and to enact divine punishment. 

# final transformation: a man among God 
initiated by mercedes plea, who also reached out to god. Then the death of Fernand, losing the mind of Villefort, and the forgiveness of Danglars. Monte Cristo goes against the theme of revenge when speaking to Mercedes.
