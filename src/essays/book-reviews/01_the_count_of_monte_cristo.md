"And you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free." (John 8:32)

When you first pick up the Penguin Classics edition of the tome that is The Count of Monte Cristo you will meet a back cover that reads: "The epic tale of wrongful imprisonment, adventure and revenge, in its definitive translation." This misrepresents Dumas' classic, though, as this book is not about revenge. Instead, it is a story of forgiveness, repentence, and faith. 

The initial wrongful imprisonment, and subsequent plot for revenge, only serves as a catalyst for Dantes' spiritual liberation-a two part transformation beyond the confines of the material world: first into the worldy Count of Monte Cristo, an international multi-millionaire and master of disguise, and then, finally, into a faithful servant of God. Edmond Dantes was born a prisoner to sin: His incarceration saved his soul ('As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good' Genesis 50:20)

## Mercedes tames the lion 
mercedes makes him realize that he isn't acting on behalf of himself but instead he's acting on behalf of himself. This is the first time that he's humbled outside of prison and reforms his life. 

The count also uses scripture to justify his action against his enemies children. After this second transformation, though, he is willing to help all of the enemies of his children. He helps Albert get an opportunity to work, he gives eugenie a passport to flee with her lover, and he makes sure that Valentine survives. 

how can we say that he wasn't taking revenge?

## Fernand 
His revenge is in inquiry. He only reveals the truth about Fernand, which is enough for Albert and Mercedes to abandon him without remorse. In the end he commits suicide, not because of the Count, but because of his own actions being revealed. He was fine living with the lie for the rest of his life. Pride. 

## Villefort
Villefort thinks he is God, essentially. He wants to be the hammer of justice, and he enjoys punishing others. He acts as if he is doing a great moral duty but he is actually a sick person. 

Villefort's demise comes in two parts, one because of his affair with madame danglars and the subsequent child who lived after they buried him alive. And then the other woman that he married carryign to much for son's ransom that she was willing to kill multiple people in the household, finally leading to her killing herself and her son. 

Monte Cristo influenced Villefort's wife but it was Villefort's actions that lead to his demise. Wrath. 


## Danglars 
Danglers punishment comes as a result of him fleeing paris. He is willing to flee his wife and daughter because his portfolio is running out of money. His success largely came from insider trading, rather than actual ability, and once that insider knowledge was removed, he no longer was able to make money. If he never flees Paris, he would not have been captured by Luigi Vampa. Monte Cristo still forgives him, though, the moment he repents in earnest. Before that he is a self made victim. Envy: his willingness to betray Albert because he thought that he could make more money by marrying Eugenie to Cavalcatti. 

## A final message in two parts 
initiated by mercedes plea, who also reached out to god. Then the death of Fernand, losing the mind of Villefort, and the forgiveness of Danglars. Monte Cristo goes against the theme of revenge when speaking to Mercedes.
